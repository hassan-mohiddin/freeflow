# F5 on Haiku: requirements withheld or given

Run on 2026-10-12 at commit `5a973db9`'s suite (`groups/`, 50 groups), Review Work from the working tree, subject and judge `anthropic/claude-haiku-5-5` (thinking medium) with `@gotgenes/pi-anthropic-auth@3.4.2`. Each group ran 3 independent trials: 300 reviews. Baseline arm: no requirements. Candidate arm: the change's requirements as declared context. Scored with `tools/score-suite.mjs`; trial and aggregate IDs are in `runs.tsv` beside this note.

## Result

Giving the reviewer the requirements roughly doubles how often it finds a defect, on tuning and held-out cases alike, and makes it block more often. It does not make it pass clean changes: Haiku almost never passes anything, with or without requirements.

**Defect identified** (90 defective reviews per arm; judged by the advisory judge, see Measurement):

| Slice | Strict: yes in both label orders | Lenient: yes in one order, no in neither |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All | 42/90 → 69/90 | 48/90 → 80/90 |
| Tuning (20 groups) | 29/60 → 47/60 | 31/60 → 53/60 |
| Held out (10 groups) | 13/30 → 22/30 | 17/30 → 27/30 |
| Logic (20) | 29/60 → 42/60 | 34/60 → 52/60 |
| Convention (5) | 3/15 → 13/15 | 4/15 → 14/15 |
| Security (5) | 10/15 → 14/15 | 10/15 → 14/15 |
| Real past defects (5) | 2/15 → 11/15 | 2/15 → 13/15 |

Per group (3 trials each arm), the candidate identified the defect more often in 19 groups, less often in 5, and equally in 6 (strict; sign test two-sided p ≈ 0.007). Lenient: 18, 4, 8 (p ≈ 0.004).

**pass^3**, a defective group caught (identified and not passed) in all three trials:

| | Strict | Lenient |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tuning | 5/20 → 12/20 | 7/20 → 15/20 |
| Held out | 2/10 → 5/10 | 4/10 → 7/10 |

**Judgments**, first line of `review.md`:

| | Pass | Non-blocking | Inconclusive | Blocking | Missing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clean, baseline (60) | 4 | 14 | 41 | 1 | 0 |
| Clean, candidate (60) | 5 | 20 | 29 | 5 | 1 |
| Defective, baseline (90) | 3 | 9 | 30 | 48 | 0 |
| Defective, candidate (90) | 0 | 7 | 8 | 75 | 0 |

A clean change passed in 9 of 120 reviews. Most clean reviews end Inconclusive, nearly always on "the author did not rerun X" or "`files/` could not be read". With requirements, Blocking on clean changes rose from 1/60 to 5/60.

**Correction (clean audit, later on 2026-10-12):** the clean side above is unreliable. An audit of the 20 clean variants (`clean-audit.json`) excluded five that hold a genuine blocking-level issue, and narrowed over-reaching requirements in the rest. Three of the five candidate Blocking reviews were of `rs-2c00f6e3`, whose clean change fails its own requirement 5, so those reviews were right. The defect-side results are unaffected: they are measured on the seeded and real variants.

## What it means for Review Work

- The requirements are the largest lever measured so far. Conventions and real past defects are almost invisible without them (3/15 and 2/15) and mostly found with them (13/15 and 11/15), because a convention or an intent violation is only a defect against a stated rule. Review Work should get the change's requirements, acceptance criteria or plan in front of the reviewer, and say so when it reviews without them.
- Clean changes are rarely passed. That is the F2 target: an Inconclusive that rests on unrerun checks the reviewer could not run is a false rejection in practice. Measure it before changing wording.
- Real past defects are the hardest class without requirements (2/15) and are the most realistic; keep them in every comparison.

## Measurement

- **The judge decides "identified".** The first review question names the defect's location, expression and effect. The line heuristic in the scorer (a finding naming the file and a line within 3) was rejected as the measure: on clean reviews it "finds" the seeded location 18/60 (baseline) and 27/59 (candidate), because seeds sit in code reviewers comment on anyway.
- **Judge checks.** Same family as the subject (Haiku judging Haiku). Ran with `--swap`; the two label orders disagreed on the first question in 56 of 300 answers, mostly a "yes" against an "unclear" over an off-by-one line citation, hence the two readings above. Ten decisive verdicts were checked by hand against the review and the seed (six yes, four no): all ten were right. 12 of 150 judge runs first failed by answering only question 1; the reply example showed one entry, and the fixed prompt (one entry per question) completed the 6 that failed a plain retry.
- **The judge's view is capped** at 60,000 characters of materials and 20,000 per file. A whole `review.md` always fits, so "identified" is answerable; the second question (unsupported findings) is weaker on large changes and is not reported.
- **Reviewers could not list `files/`.** Groups allow only `read` and `write`; 61 of 300 reviews (23 baseline, 38 candidate) say `files/` could not be read and fall back on `change.diff`. This lowers absolute rates in both arms. The groups now also allow `ls` and `find`, so later runs are not directly comparable with these.
- **Security** has 5 defective variants (3 seeded, 2 real), below the 6 seeded planned; its rates are descriptive.
- One subject wrote its review only in its reply, not to `review.md` (counted as missing).
- Haiku only. Nothing here says how a stronger model behaves.
