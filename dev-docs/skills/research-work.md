# Research Work

> **Covers:** `skills/research-work/`
> **Tests:** `.skill-eval/research-work/tests/`
> **Verified at:** `c03af300` (2026-10-28)

For anyone changing Research Work: what it is meant to change, why it is shaped the way it is, and what the evidence shows. The method itself is in [the skill](../../skills/research-work/SKILL.md); this record holds the reasons it does not.

## Purpose

Research Work is read when a question has to be answered from evidence gathered and checked first. It carries the question to a claim the evidence supports and on to where the claim is used. It has two routes: the in-depth report, a question answered from code at a pinned commit, papers or docs; and the study, a question answered by runs planned and locked before the data, with the quick check as the study at `check` rigor. It also owns the general method of study design and measurement, and the statistics for any result that comes from a sample, which Evaluate Behavior uses.

It exists because agents know the textbook methods and fail at applying them:
- they answer from summaries and memory;
- they cite whole files, or a branch of the code as the whole path;
- they compute intervals and tests by hand, unpaired where the data is paired, with normal intervals on small samples;
- they start running before there is a design, choose the analysis after seeing the data, and rerun failures until they pass;
- they hedge every sentence instead of stating each claim at the strength its evidence has.

The skill is mostly structure (formats, checks, a level per claim) and decisions, not exposition.

## Behavioral Claim

- **Behavior it produces:**
  - a research brief agreed with the user before the first source is read;
  - every source pinned and recorded with how it was checked;
  - every claim registered with its level, capped by its sources' checking, before the prose states it (at study and publication rigor);
  - a report whose citations exist at the pinned commit, whose quotes appear in their sources and whose numbers trace to the register;
  - statistics from `stats.mjs`, reported with their method and interval;
  - for a study, a plan answering the twelve questions, locked with the user's agreement before the runs that count; a receipt for every run, failed ones included; each deviation logged with who cleared it; and confirmatory claims only for the locked plan's hypotheses.
- **Failure it prevents:**
  - claims stronger than their sources;
  - unchecked citations;
  - numbers that appear nowhere in the evidence;
  - wrong intervals and unpaired tests;
  - a hypothesis or analysis chosen after the data, and a failed run dropped from the record;
  - a model judge's verdicts counted without evidence the judge agrees with known answers;
  - caveats stacked into every sentence.
- **Earliest decision:** before the first search or read of sources.
- **Cues before the body is read:**
  - its description;
  - one line in core naming it outside the cycle ("Research Work when a question has to be answered from evidence gathered and checked first …"), added with the skill.
- **Guaranteed context:** the description on every host; core where Freeflow's runtime prompts are on.
- **Nearby cases it leaves alone:**
  - comparing options from facts in hand (Discuss Work);
  - checking that a change works (Verify Work);
  - running an agent eval (Evaluate Behavior, which uses its statistics);
  - a single lookup.
- **When it stops steering:** when the report and its check output are handed to the user.

## Design

- **One trunk for all research.** Research Work is one skill with a route per application, not a skill per application. Each skill boundary between a request and the method lowers the chance the method reaches the decision (Write Skill, "Choose Skill Boundaries").
  - ARS, the academic-research skill pack studied before this design, spreads the same ground over five skills, 35 modes and a fixed ten-stage pipeline.
  - This version ships the in-depth report, the study (with the quick check as the study at `check` rigor) and the paper, written from the register and answered in a rebuttal ledger. The body names no route that is not built, since the package holds no authoring status.
- **The paper route** (task record P64) keeps the design's writing techniques in `references/paper.md`, each with its basis (guide, measured or one case). Three formats carry its checks: the paper outline (a proposition, contributions with claim IDs that must exist in the register, one sentence per planned paragraph, figures with data files that must exist, objections and where each is answered), the rebuttal ledger (a choice and reason per reviewer comment, a fulfilled pointer for each commitment before submission, and the goalpost guard: a later-round comment is either new or continues an earlier one, and the new ones are listed), and the evidence table for a literature review (levels capped by how each source was checked). The proposition is checked at review, not before drafting; the draft gets one review, never a loop against a model judge; revision is by patch; venue checklists are fetched at use.
- **The source and plan checks** (task record P72) close the measured gaps nearest the sources: deep-research systems' citation accuracy ran from 31% to 79% across nine systems (DeepTRACE), and 48% of non-compliant repository citations were omissions (arXiv:2512.12117).
  - `sources.mjs lookup` goes online only when asked, as D-008 settled: a DOI through Crossref with the notices that update it (a retraction, a correction), an arXiv ID through arXiv, a URL's page. A record verifies on its title and an author the Source names, both matched as whole words with accents folded, as P72 agreed; a Source that names no title stays unresolved, and a page verifies when it carries most of the Source's words. It uses `curl`, which honours the proxy and CA settings every host already has, and falls back to `fetch`. Answers are cached by identifier and Source, with the match rule's version, so a rerun is offline and a changed rule asks again; a service that did not answer is not cached. It reports what each row's Lookup should read and never edits the ledger. A record that matches the Source only in part is `unresolved`, not `refuted`: refuting is kept for an identifier plainly resolving to another work, so a vague Source line cannot make a real paper look fabricated.
  - `sources.mjs reads` takes the ranges a worker read from its Claude Code transcript (Read calls, and `sed -n`, `cat`, `head`, `tail -n +N`, `nl` and `git show` in Bash, following each command's `cd` and the transcript's working folder; a pipe into `sed -n`, `head` or `cat -n` narrows the range, and output piped through `grep` or written to a file is not a read), and `cite-check --reads` fails a code citation with any line no read covers. A read of a clone's files counts only while the clone is at the pinned commit, as it is when `reads` runs, since the transcript does not say where it was then. Another host's transcript needs its own reader, or a reads file written in the same shape.
  - The lock records the git commit that holds the plan when git tracks it, committed as it stands; `check` refuses a lock whose commit is not in HEAD's history or does not hold the locked plan. A task folder is usually ignored by git, so its plans are locked without an anchor, and `check` says so rather than failing.
  - The plan's validation refuses a rule that stops when a result turns significant (unless it names a sequential test, not negated) and a confirmatory hypothesis whose plan states no power computation, with its numbers or its `stats.mjs power` command, or why none applies. Both read the wording, so they catch the usual forms, not every one.
- **Rechecking others' numbers** (task record P74) gives the checks ARS keeps as receipts (GRIM, GRIMMER, p from the statistic and df) a table: each row transcribes a reported result with its labels, each number as printed, and `stats.mjs recheck` writes its Status from a closed list, so a status cannot be typed in by hand: validation recomputes it. A value with d decimals stands for its rounding interval, so a tie rounded either way passes, as the Statistical heartburn audit did. GRIMMER is checked exactly: a search over whole numbers for one dataset with the printed mean and SD, since the parity rule alone passed impossible SDs at n of 2 to 4 (the review below); a search too large to run is not computable, never a pass. The methods are reimplemented from their published descriptions (GRIM, DEBIT, statcheck's rules) and, for GRIMMER, from its definition, since Anaya's preprint was not read; statcheck is GPL-3 and no code is borrowed. A mismatch keeps the claim that leans on the number at L1; it is evidence about the report, not a verdict on the study, since one may be a typo.
- **Several seeds** (task record P75) are aggregated as Agarwal et al. (2021) recommend for few runs on several tasks: the interquartile mean of all runs pooled, with the mean and median of the per-task means and the optimality gap alongside (as rliable's metrics.py computes them, read for this check), each with a stratified bootstrap interval that resamples runs within each task; and the probability of improvement, the per-task chance a run of one method beats a run of the other, averaged over tasks. rliable implements them under Apache-2.0; the methods are reimplemented from the paper. pass^k gets an interval: for one task the exact interval for the pass rate raised to k, which keeps its coverage because p^k rises with p; for a suite a two-stage bootstrap that resamples tasks and then each task's trials, since a bootstrap over tasks alone left out the trial noise: it covered about 70% at 3 tasks and was zero width for identical tasks (task record T39). Results warn when a task has fewer than 5 runs, or a suite fewer than 10 tasks.
- **Fields that check guidance** (task record P75, T35 to T38). Three pieces of existing guidance get checks: a claim worded as an absence cites the search behind it (`search:Q<n>`), since novelty is only as good as the search; a plan's analysis variants (`V<n>`) must each be reported by some claim, since an analysis run and dropped is the forking path the plan was meant to close; and the plan declares the models in its method with their snapshots and access dates. The new plan sections are optional and noted when absent, so plans locked before them stay valid. The Evidence Table may name each study's weakness. Two items from the not-built list were already covered and are not rebuilt: the literature review's counts (the search ledger records every hit with its state and counts them) and a list of conclusions a claim does not license (the register's Limit and the report's Limits).
- **The paper tools** (task record D-014) make the paper's parts from checked inputs by recorded commands, since generated papers fail at figures (missing or misplaced in 4 of 7 in Beel et al.; unreadable plots in AI Scientist), references (21 of 68 still wrong after three integrity rounds in ARS's own showcase) and builds that live only in prose:
  - `figure.mjs` draws an SVG from a spec and a data file, refusing values typed into the spec, with no dependencies so it runs on any host. The outline's Figures row names the receipt that drew it, and validation compares the receipt's input and output hashes with the files as they are now, so a stale figure shows.
  - `paper.mjs run` writes the same receipt as `study.mjs run` for a paper with no study plan; both use `recordRun` in `lib/study.mjs`.
  - `claims.mjs draft-check` holds the checks a reader would catch (abstract numbers missing from the body, citations and references that resolve to nothing, figures without receipts, word limits) and lists, without failing, undefined acronyms and process words. One command holds every reference check, so `trace` stays the number check.
  - `sources.mjs bib` writes the bibliography from the ledger and marks every source not verified; full author fields come from a reference manager's export matched by DOI or arXiv ID, not from a lookup (online lookups are a later step).
  - `paper.mjs anchor` and `apply` make revision by patch mechanical: a manifest of the reviewed draft's blocks, a patch whose changes each name a finding, and an apply report.
  - `paper.mjs build` runs Pandoc with the venue's template fetched at use; for PDF a LaTeX engine or a headless Chromium. Nothing is shipped or installed. `check-pdf` reads pages and words with poppler's tools when present.
  - `artifact.mjs init project` lays out a research folder; paths stay relative, so it is a default, not a requirement.
- **Where research artifacts live** depends on the work (task record P69): research done for another task stays in that task's folder, a task that is itself research uses the project's research folder, and a quick question answered from facts in hand is not research. Research Work owns the rule; Write Docs points to it.
- **Boundaries:**
  - Discuss Work owns agreeing the brief and the user's choices.
  - Review Work owns how any review runs. Research Work supplies the brief, the ledgers, the register and the check output to the reviewer.
  - Research Work owns the report's shape (Research Report, in `references/artifacts.md`, moved from Write Docs by task record decision D-006, its strength now the evidence level). Write Docs owns every document's header and routes research documents here.
  - Evaluate Behavior owns running agent evals and imports `skills/research-work/scripts/lib/stats.mjs` by relative path (`lib/agreement.mjs` and `lib/decision.mjs` re-export it). Its `evaluatorVersion()` hashes `stats.mjs` too, so a change to the statistics invalidates reuse keys.
  - The general method of study design and measurement is Research Work's (`references/study.md`, `measurement.md`): claim classes, the decision rule first, sizing, the noise floor, held-out items, controls for mechanical checks, reading before counting, fair tasks, judges, detectors measured on both sides, cost. Evaluate Behavior's `design-and-measurement.md` points to it and keeps its binding Rules and what is particular to agents: its claim mapping with "Ready", the suite `decision` and its sign test, trial schedules, `component: []` noise checks, its judge flags and the seeded-defect recipe. `skill-author compare --all-units` against the merge base found 169 units: 137 kept, 9 edited, 16 moved, 6 reworded and 1 unmatched, which moved to `measurement.md`. The three rule-like rewordings were read for meaning, and one lost words that were then restored; the edited and moved units are Evaluate Behavior's own sections, rewritten to point here. The output, the verdict and the meaning checks are in the building task's `evidence/study-route/`. Kept text shows the text survived, not that behavior did.
  - When a study's arms are agent configurations on a host, Evaluate Behavior runs them; the body's routing table says so.
  - Judges have one owner for each part (task record D-013). Research Work's `measurement.md` says what any judge needs before its answers count, and the judge validation record holds it. Review Work's Judge Contract records each use, with the same model rule, and counts a judge as calibrated only with a passing record, repeatability included; advisory review needs no record. Evaluate Behavior keeps its evaluator's judge mechanics, and `review --calibrate` fills the record's agreement and kappa (the interval comes from `stats.mjs`).
  - A question answered by a measured rate or comparison comes here from Discuss Work's learning action and Execute Work's design experiments; a prototype answering a design question stays with them. Verify Work's benchmark guidance points to `stats.mjs` for an interval on a difference. Track Work names a study's plan and lock in the Working Record and does not copy its receipts or deviations.
- **The evidence scale, L0 to L5** (proposed, reported, checked, observed, compared, replicated):
  - It is Research Work's own, general enough for any domain, so *State Before Action*'s E0–E5 becomes a specialization of it.
  - How a source was checked caps the claims resting on it: a source known only from a summary, an abstract or memory supports at most L1.
  - A comparison without a decision rule fixed beforehand is exploratory, at most L3.
  - `artifact.mjs validate --sources` enforces both caps.
  - The level carries the uncertainty, so sentences state claims plainly.
  - A value computed exactly under a stated model is L2, and a comparison of computed values under a locked rule is L4 (task record P45). The test study's workers had given its one-arm threshold rules L4, which L4's definition, a controlled comparison, did not cover.
- **Rigor by stakes:** `check`, `study`, `publication`, chosen by the user in the brief. It sets which artifacts and checks are required, so a quick answer is not run at publication cost.
- **Formats as one contract, enforced by a script.**
  - `references/artifacts.md` defines the research brief, source ledger, search ledger (JSONL), claims register, study plan, run ledger, deviation log and judge validation record, and the citation forms. `artifact.mjs` creates and validates the Markdown ones; `study.mjs` alone writes the run ledger and deviation log.
  - This is Track Work's pattern (a format reference plus a script that writes and checks the file), chosen over template files in `assets/`.
  - Search-ledger counts are computed from the hit lines, never typed.
- **Mechanical checks before any review, and none that call a model:**
  - `sources.mjs cite-check` reads each `S<n>:path:line` at the commit its ledger row pins and finds each quote in its source;
  - `claims.mjs trace` matches every number and claim ID outside code in the report to the register.
  
  Anything they cannot check is listed as not checked, never as passed. The body also says what passing does not show, because the first real run passed all 93 citations while three of five checked claims dropped a guard condition.
- **A study's gates are scripts.** Each of the three binding gates in the design is a refusal, not an instruction:
  - *The plan is locked before the runs that count.* `study.mjs lock` records the plan's sha256 (over everything but its Amendments section), who locked it and its hypotheses. A later change is an amendment with a reason; `study.mjs check` refuses a plan changed without one. An amendment after study runs exist records every hypothesis it adds or changes, and `artifact.mjs validate --plan` then refuses those as confirmatory; a planned count it cuts still binds the check. A study confirms only what its locked plan said it would test.
  - *Every number traces to a command.* `study.mjs run` writes a receipt per command: program and its hash, input and output hashes, exit code, duration, output tails, plan hash. A non-zero exit is data, a command that could not start is recorded, and each planned trial number needs a started receipt carrying it. `claims.mjs trace --results` finds each register number among the result file's values (a JSON file's numbers, not its strings): presence, not that the analysis computed it.
  - *A judge's verdict counts only with a validation record.* The record holds agreement with a gold set and its interval against a lower bound fixed before scoring, both answer orders, length control, decoys, repeatability, which model judged which (a different model by default, another family optional; the same model only with the reason, since a user may have one subscription and one model; task record P43) and the rubric's hash; validation refuses a `pass` the numbers do not support, and a register row citing `judge:<record>` that does not pass is capped at L1.
  - A pilot before the lock is not seen by any check: the plan says what it showed and who saw it, and a hypothesis written after a pilot's result is exploratory (task record P46).
  - Deviations are logged with who cleared them, and an uncleared one holds `study.mjs check`. The log names the clearer, so a self-clearance shows; nothing stops one (task record, question 4 of the Evaluate Behavior split). A derived step that fails, such as a validity check, holds the analysis only once it is logged as a deviation.
  - *Held-out items stay held out.* A planned run may name its split (`tuning` or `held-out`); each receipt records it, and `study.mjs check` refuses any run made before the lock on a unit the plan marks held-out, whatever split it was given then (the method check's sealed-holdout design).
  - Both ledgers are hash-chained (each line carries the hash of the line before), so an edited, removed or reordered receipt shows at `study.mjs check` (the ARS review's hash-chained ledger).
  - The records are plain files: these checks catch slips and partial edits, not a deliberate rewrite (a lock edited by hand, a ledger recomputed from its first line). `study.md` says to commit them as the study goes, so the repository's history is the audit. Anchoring them outside the folder is in `sources/not-built.md`.
- **Statistics in one place, guidance at the decision.**
  - `stats.mjs` has the methods agents get wrong when computing by hand:
    - Wilson and exact intervals;
    - exact McNemar and the sign test;
    - paired differences and sign-flip permutation;
    - cluster-robust means and cluster bootstrap;
    - kappa (also weighted and prevalence-adjusted) and Krippendorff's alpha;
    - Holm's correction;
    - unbiased pass@k and pass^k;
    - TOST;
    - exact McNemar power and paired sample size.
  - `interval clustered-bayes`: the hierarchical beta-binomial of Gelman et al. (*Bayesian Data Analysis*, 3rd ed., section 5.3), integrated on a grid, for pass or fail values in few clusters. Bowyer et al. report that only a Bayesian clustered model reached nominal coverage there (read through the web research, L1); the model is the textbook one, not theirs, whose code has no licence and whose priors were not read.
  - Resampling records its seed. Each method is tested against a published worked example (Newcombe 1998 for Wilson) or values computed once with scipy, statsmodels and errorbars, none of which is a dependency.
  - The body's "Numbers From Samples" table maps the data's shape to the method and names the wrong default each replaces.
  - Methods are reimplemented from papers and textbooks, and code is borrowed only under MIT or Apache (task record decision D-008).
- **References by what is read together.** Six references: framing for a question not yet answerable as written, investigation and claims for the report route's Survey and Conclude, `study.md` for designing and running a study (study design and running were read one after the other on every study, so they are one file), `measurement.md` for a measure, a judge or a detector inside or outside a study, and `artifacts.md`, the format contract. `skill-author compare --all-units` against main found 483 units: 462 kept verbatim, 14 edited (link retargets), 3 moved, 4 reworded (the merged opening and Goal lines, read for meaning) and 0 unmatched. The output and the meaning checks are in the building task's `evidence/compaction/`. The references link each other in a cycle (artifacts, measurement and study), as they did before the merge: each points to the others for a neighbouring situation, and none is needed to reach another from the body.
- **Workers return files, or text when the host refuses files.** Claude Code refuses report files from a subagent, so a worker returns the content for the coordinator to save unchanged (seen twice in the task that built the skill).

## Rejected

- **ARS's structure** (five skills, 35 modes, a fixed pipeline, review panels that must propose fixes). Descriptions compete, fixed stages spend compute that changes no decision, and forced fixes produced caveat-dense drafts.
- **The statistics table in `stats.mjs --help` instead of the body** (considered while compacting the references). The failure it prevents is computing by hand without running `stats.mjs`, and that agent never sees the script's help; in the body, it sees the table.
- **Templates in `assets/`.** A format reference with a validating script keeps one owner and checks the file later.
- **Python with scipy.** Not guaranteed on Freeflow's hosts.
- **A vendored copy of the statistics in Evaluate Behavior.** AGENTS.md allows no second maintained copy.
- **A model as the judge of whether a citation exists.** Model verifiers' false-positive rates ran from 0.050 to 0.478 in HALLMARK (arXiv:2607.18360); a failed lookup is "unresolved", never "fabricated".

## Dropped

- **A check that flags wording stronger than a claim's level.** It waits for evidence that agents overclaim in reports. The first run's failure was dropped conditions, not strong verbs.
- **Defining "too large for one context" for splitting work across workers.** Left as judgment.
- **From the test study's notes:** a range form for the Planned runs table (one study needed 189 rows; not durable enough yet); a rule for naming pilot units (the pilot's own naming worked); where an unattended brief marks its defaults (minor); and removing "Wald undercovers" from the statistics table and `stats.mjs` because it states a result without its source (the advice is supported, by this study on n = 10 to 100 and by Bowyer et al. at N = 100, and the evidence belongs here, not in the package).
