# Learning Action

Read this when existing evidence cannot settle a material question and running something could: before proposing an experiment or prototype, while running one, and when its evidence comes back.

This reference is judgment: adapt it to the question. Its binding statements restate [Discuss Work](../SKILL.md) or [Execute Work](../../execute-work/SKILL.md) Rules.

## Decide Whether To Run One

Use an experiment or prototype when existing evidence cannot settle a material question and exercising behavior can distinguish the remaining answers. A separate prototype should isolate the uncertainty more safely or economically than changing production; unfamiliarity alone does not require one.

If the work is an accepted useful outcome regardless of what it teaches, treat it as delivery work, not an experiment; do not disguise implementation as learning. Incidental learning does not change its authority or acceptance boundary.

## Frame It

Frame the action before proposing it:

- the question and its consequence for the accepted outcome or approach;
- plausible answers and the observation that would distinguish them;
- the smallest adequate prototype or observer;
- permitted effects, environment, and production exclusion;
- expected evidence and return conditions: question answered, observing limit reached, or further work requiring a different question or expanded scope;
- what would justify discarding, revising, or proposing promotion.

Add a time or cost boundary when repetition or setup could grow; do not demand an exact token prediction. If no adequate observer is available within scope, state the limitation rather than inventing a proof.

Check that the agreement covers the framed action, preserve it through [Track Work](../../track-work/SKILL.md) when needed, then run it through Execute Work. Existing authority may already cover the experiment; its name does not create another approval gate.

## Run It

Answer the one declared question with the least building that can, within the limits the framing set. Report a negative or inconclusive result as plainly as a positive one, and do not polish a prototype toward a favorable answer. A successful demonstration is not production acceptance: never promote exploratory code into production unless that promotion is deliberately selected and its effects authorized.

## Assess What It Showed

Assess returned evidence against the original question. Negative or inconclusive evidence is a valid result; one failed technique does not prove every alternative impossible.

Revise only the affected understanding. When continuity matters, preserve the consequence for the task, the remaining uncertainty, and what happens to the exploratory artifacts, as [discussion continuity](discussion-continuity.md) describes. Stop assessing when the supported next direction or the unresolved decision is clear.
